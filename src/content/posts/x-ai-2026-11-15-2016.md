5. **Gorden Sun：Meta 与纽约大学的视觉编码拆解研究**：Ellis Brown、Chris Hoang 等人的论文 *Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding*（arXiv:2611.17334）在同一数据、骨架和训练配方下，把视觉编码器拆成三项选择：单独预训练带来的先验、专供视觉的参数，以及这些参数放在融合之前还是模型内部。主要结论：编码器不必先单独预训练，和主模型从零一起训，看图问答可以打平；但必须保留视觉专用参数，encoder-free 方案即便把参数加回主干，也大约要 3 倍算力才能追上平均 VQA；把视觉模块嵌进主干前八层（in-fusion），效果可与前置编码器相当，并且能降低首字延迟。论文在一块 H200 上、1.7B 主干、16 路并发（每路一张 448 像素图加 256 个文本 token）测得：0.4B 视觉预算下，前置编码器首字约 64 毫秒，in-fusion 约 37 毫秒。项目页：https://unbundling.github.io/ https://x.com/Gorden_Sun/status/2121806966062485776
> [en] **Gorden Sun on a Meta and NYU study that unbundles vision encoding**: Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang and colleagues, in *Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding* (arXiv:2611.17334), hold data, backbone and recipe fixed and split a vision encoder into three choices: a prior from separate pretraining, dedicated vision parameters, and whether that capacity sits before fusion or inside the backbone. The main findings: separate encoder pretraining is optional — training the encoder from scratch with the language model can match VQA; dedicated vision parameters matter — encoder-free models need about 3× the compute to match average VQA even when the backbone is given the extra parameters; and putting vision-only modules in the first eight backbone layers (in-fusion) is comparable to a pre-fusion encoder and can be faster to serve. On one H200, a 1.7B backbone and 16 concurrent requests (one 448-px image and 256 text tokens each), time to first token was about 64 ms for a 0.4B pre-fusion encoder and about 37 ms for in-fusion. Project page: https://unbundling.github.io/

4. **Bojie Li：照抄 Moshi 全双工做语音，工具调用会变慢**：他引用 @neuralwarlock 对 Moshi 架构瓶颈的分析，并结合自己做过之后的判断。核心观点是 thinking cannot be done in speech：Moshi 把文本 token 和即将播出的音频帧锁成一对一，模型没法在不推进音频时钟的情况下先写出一长段思考或工具调用；若用静音硬撑，用户就会听到明显空白。因此现在的类 Moshi 全双工方案（他举了 GPT-Live 1 和 TML interaction model）往往把工具调用交给后台慢思考模型，来回两次都会叠上延迟。他认为更接近人的做法是三段式：Semantic VAD 负责把连续声波稀疏成「何时该唤起 LLM」（不是「何时开口」，开口由 LLM 决定），再接多模态 LLM 和流式 TTS，由 LLM 决定是否开说、停说、改口或去调工具。他称这套「对连续信号做稀疏化」的思路也适用于视觉和 computer use。https://x.com/bojie_li/status/2121903370609062376
> [en] **Bojie Li: copying Moshi-style full duplex makes tool use slow in voice**: quoting @neuralwarlock’s analysis of Moshi’s architectural bottlenecks, and adding what he learned from building one, he argues that thinking cannot be done in speech. Moshi locks each text token to an audio frame that is spoken right away, so the model cannot write a burst of reasoning or a tool call without advancing the audio clock; padding that with silence still leaves the user in dead air. As a result, Moshi-like full-duplex stacks (he cites GPT-Live 1 and the TML interaction model) often delegate tool calls to a slower backend reasoner, and the round trips add latency. He thinks a closer match to humans is a three-stage setup: a semantic VAD that sparsifies the acoustic stream into when the LLM should be invoked (not when to start speaking — the LLM decides that), plus a multimodal LLM and streaming TTS, with the LLM choosing to start, stop, self-correct or call a tool. He says the same idea of sparsifying a continuous signal also applies to vision and computer use.
